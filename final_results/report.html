Radar–camera MVP: comparative experiment

This experiment tests complementarity; it does not assume fusion wins. Ten samples from two scenes excluded from evaluation-model training are used. These scenes were previously inspected, so this is exploratory validation.

Darkening and Gaussian blur are controlled image perturbations, not simulated physical rain. Radar remains unchanged. Camera-unavailable tests sensor outage. Actual radar degradation and adverse-weather robustness are not established.

Reference labels are provisional radar XY associations. Scores compare classifications on the same labeled radar candidates. Camera results are conditional associations, not detection precision or mAP. Candidates and frames are correlated. A box can influence multiple candidates. Abstentions count against correct-over-all; conditional accuracy excludes abstentions.

Selection policy and interpretation

The camera category takes priority, including conflicts. Multiple boxes are resolved by the highest camera score among supported categories. Radar supplies a fallback when no supported camera prediction exists. Uncertainty remains only when neither source supplies a category. This policy increases coverage but may propagate camera errors. Both camera-only and fusion use the same box-selection rule for a fair comparison.

Radar clustering analysis

Clusters and isolated returns are evaluated on original images only to avoid counting the same radar predictions four times. These are different populations: a better cluster result is evidence of association with performance, not a causal ablation of DBSCAN.

cluster: 18/39 correct (46.15%).

isolated_return: 7/55 correct (12.73%).

Distance, radial structure and multiple returns can supply useful radar features. However, the present model is weak on several categories. Camera outage demonstrates fallback capability; improvement under real rain requires separate data. No thresholds were selected to force fusion to win.
